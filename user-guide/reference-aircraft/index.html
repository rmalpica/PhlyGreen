    Reference Aircraft¶

    PhlyGreen ships two reference aircraft, both built from published data:

    ATR 42-600 Airbus A320-200
    Role regional turboprop; baseline of every regional example (kerosene, hybrid, hydrogen) short-haul jet; validation of the Turbofan configuration
    Config examples/common.py: traditional_config() examples/common.py: turbofan_config()
    Example examples/28_reference_atr42.py examples/29_reference_a320.py
    Sources ATR factsheet Airbus ACAP, EASA TCDS (+ secondary sources, labelled)
    Provenance this page this page and trunk/validation/a320_reference.md

    The published data set the requirements: take-off field, climb performance, mission, payload. The model then predicts take-off weight, empty weight, wing area and installed power, which are compared with the published values. Nothing is tuned to the published masses or power. Both cases teach the same lesson, so it is stated up front:

    Minimum installed power is not minimum take-off weight

    Constraint.FindDesignPoint picks, by default, the wing loading that minimises the installed power (or thrust). Real aircraft sit at a higher wing loading: a smaller wing gives less drag and less structure, up to the limit set by the landing field and approach speed. Each reference is therefore compared at two design points:

    • A, the default (minimum installed power);
    • B, the real or landing-limited wing loading.

    1. ATR 42-600¶

    Sources¶

    ATR, ATR 42-600 factsheet (2020), https://www.atr-aircraft.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Factsheets_-_ATR_42-600.pdf. Values used (as printed):

    Results¶

    Quantity Factsheet A: default W/S (260 kg/m²) B: real W/S (341 kg/m²)
    MTOW 18,600 kg 17,843 kg (−4.1 %) 18,281 kg (−1.7 %)
    OEW 11,550 kg 11,214 kg (−2.9 %) 11,568 kg (+0.2 %)
    Wing area 54.5 m² 68.6 m² (+25.9 %) 53.6 m² (−1.7 %)
    Installed power vs 2 × 2,160 SHP 3.22 MW 3.11 MW (−3.5 %) 3.60 MW (+11.7 %)
    Installed power vs 2 × 2,400 SHP 3.58 MW 3.11 MW (−13.2 %) 3.60 MW (+0.5 %)

    At the real wing loading (B), MTOW, OEW and wing area are within 2 % of the factsheet. The sized power matches the 2,400-SHP one-engine / max-continuous rating within 0.5 %. The model sizes one engine rating per aircraft, so it is compared with both published ratings.

    The constraint diagram shows why A and B differ. The default design point sits near the minimum of the AEO-climb requirement. The real aircraft sits further right, just inside the landing wall, at a slightly higher power loading.

    Why the requirements matter so much

    An earlier version of the example constraints (950 m field, AEO climb at 210 KCAS, ≈ 2,400 ft/min initial climb) asked for ≈ 30 % more power than the real aircraft (4.2 MW). For a kerosene aircraft that is a modest penalty. For a fuel-cell aircraft, whose stack and thermal-management masses scale with power and whose heat is ≈ 1.5× its power, it inflated the take-off weight by ≈ 40 %. Check the power requirement against a real aircraft before reading anything into an electrified design.

    2. Hydrogen fuel-cell variant of the ATR 42¶

    hydrogen_config() keeps the ATR 42 airframe requirements, mission and payload and replaces the powertrain:

    Input Value
    Fuel LH₂, Ef = 141.87 MJ/kg (HHV, the basis of the fuel-cell efficiency)
    Fuel cell PEMFC_GoodPerformance (80 °C, 1.5 bar, λ = 2); design cell voltage 0.5 V; rated 2.5 A/cm²
    Stack / BoP 2,000 W/kg stack power density; BoP 40 % of the stack mass
    Electric drive motor 10 kW/kg, PMAD 10 kW/kg; η motor 0.96, PMAD 0.99, gearbox 0.96, propeller 0.85
    Storage gravimetric index 0.35 (usable H₂ / (H₂ + tank)); tank holds load + reserve
    Heat to the TMS product-water vapour fraction from the cathode-exhaust saturation (see Hydrogen)
    TMS Class I (5 kW/kg of heat) or Class II (geometric, see Thermal Management)
    Result TMS Class I TMS Class II
    MTOW 21.7 t 22.4 t
    Fuel-cell rating 3.9 MW 4.0 MW
    Fuel-cell system 2.41 t 2.50 t
    TMS 0.96 t 1.11 t

    Comparison with published fuel-cell regional designs¶

    Study Aircraft Payload Design range MTOW
    Svensson, Oliveira, Grönstedt, Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 61 (2024) 650, doi:10.1016/j.ijhydene.2024.02.382 ATR 42 resized, all fuel cell 5,500 kg (50 pax) 648 nm + reserves 21.05 t
    Eissele et al., Aerospace 10 (2023) 277, doi:10.3390/aerospace10030277 50 pax, fuel cell + battery 5,300 kg 800 km (432 nm) + reserves 19.6 t
    Schlittenhardt et al., Aerospace 12 (2025) 249, doi:10.3390/aerospace12030249 70 pax, distributed propulsion 6,650 kg 800 km (432 nm) 23.64 t
    ATI FlyZero, Zero-carbon emission aircraft concepts, FZO-AIN-REP-0007 (2022), FZR-1E 75 pax, 6 propulsors 7,875 kg 800 nm 28.8 t
    PhlyGreen (this variant) 48 pax, twin 4,560 kg 750 nm + 220 nm diversion 21.7 / 22.4 t

    The take-off weight is close to the comparable 50-seat designs. The ratio MTOW/payload is ≈ 25 % higher (4.8–4.9 against 3.6–3.8), for two reasons. The payload is smaller, and the mission is longer: 750 nm with a 220 nm diversion, against at most 648 nm with a 100 nm alternate in the others. Run with Svensson et al.'s mission inputs (648 nm + 100 nm, 5,500 kg, gravimetric index 0.555), PhlyGreen gives 22.6 t (TMS Class I) and 23.1 t (Class II), i.e. 7–10 % above their 21.05 t.

    4. Reproducing¶

    cd trunk
python examples/28_reference_atr42.py      # ATR 42-600: inputs, comparison, figures
python examples/29_reference_a320.py       # A320-200: inputs, both design points, figures
python validation/a320_reference.py        # full A320 validation (OEW calibration, payload-range)
cd .. && python docs/scripts/make_figures.py   # regenerate every figure on this page
