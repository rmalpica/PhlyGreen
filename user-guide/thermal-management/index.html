    Thermal Management (Fuel-Cell TMS)¶

    A PEM fuel cell converts roughly half of the hydrogen energy into electricity; the rest is low-temperature heat that the thermal-management system (TMS) must reject. PhlyGreen offers two TMS models for the fuel-cell configurations ('Hydrogen' and 'FuelCellBattery'), selected with TMSConfig(tms_class=...) (legacy key TMSInput['TMS Class']):

    Class I (default) Class II
    Model specific power geometric heat exchangers (FC_SPEC architecture)
    Mass \(Q_{\text{peak}} / \text{specific power}\) exchanger cores + pump + pump motor
    Drag none ram-air duct drag, added to the drag polar
    Power none coolant pump, an electrical load on the fuel cell
    Needs nothing CoolProp
    Cost negligible ≈ 0.3 s per TMS sizing, ≈ 1 min per aircraft design

    1. Using the Class-II TMS¶

    Typed configuration¶

    Attach a TMSConfig to the AircraftConfig of a fuel-cell aircraft. Every field except tms_class is optional; see Inputs for the defaults.

    import PhlyGreen as pg
from PhlyGreen.config import TMSConfig
from common import hydrogen_config          # examples/common.py: the ATR-like hydrogen aircraft

config = hydrogen_config()
config.tms = TMSConfig(
    tms_class='II',                 # geometric model
    coolant='INCOMP::MEG-30%',      # any CoolProp fluid
    coolant_delta_t=10.0,           # [K] coolant temperature drop over the loop
    max_weight_per_unit=50.0,       # [kg] per heat-exchanger unit
    max_volume_per_unit=0.05,       # [m^3] per heat-exchanger unit
    ram_recovery=0.9,               # intake recovers 90 % of the dynamic pressure
)

aircraft = pg.build_aircraft()
aircraft.configure(config)          # sizes the aircraft with the Class-II TMS in the loop

r = aircraft.results()
print(f"MTOW {r.WTO:.0f} kg, TMS {r.tms_mass:.0f} kg (class {r.tms_class}), "
      f"pump {r.tms_pump_power/1e3:.1f} kW, ram drag {r.tms_ram_drag:.0f} N, "
      f"sized at '{r.tms_design_point}'")
aircraft.tms.report()

# The chosen hardware, rated at every mission point: heat to reject vs capacity.
tms, result = aircraft.tms, aircraft.weight.tms_result
for point in aircraft.mission.thermal_profile:
    q_cap, q_req = tms.RateAtPoint(result, point, capped=False)
    print(f"{point['name']:20s} required {q_req/1e3:7.0f} kW   capacity {q_cap/1e3:7.0f} kW")

    The design point is the first climb segment. It is not the point with the most heat (that is Climb2), but at 80 m/s it has the least ram air. The geometry sized there rejects the heat everywhere else with margin, while a geometry sized at Climb2 would fall short at Climb1. If even max_hex_units units cannot keep each unit within the mass/volume caps, the report flags [per-unit caps exceeded] and a warning is issued; raise max_hex_units or the caps to remove it.

    The drag-work line comes from the check of the hardware along the mission (below). Over the mission, the constant drag increment of the aircraft charges 25 times the duct drag work of the momentum balance, so the aircraft carries the duct drag conservatively. The hardware rejects the heat, stays within its pressure budgets and does not choke at any point of the mission, and the coolant-side wall stays above the coolant freezing point.

    Legacy dictionaries¶

    aircraft.DesignAircraft(AerodynamicsInput, ConstraintsInput, MissionInput, EnergyInput,
                        MissionStages, DiversionStages,
                        TMSInput={'TMS Class': 'II', 'TMS Ram Recovery': 0.9})

    Without TMSInput (or with 'TMS Class': 'I') the Class-I model is used.

    What the design exposes¶

    Where What
    aircraft.results() tms_class, tms_mass, tms_pump_power [W], tms_ram_drag [N], tms_design_point
    aircraft.weight.WHeat_Exchanger TMS mass in the take-off-weight balance (the cooling item of the mass breakdown)
    aircraft.weight.tms_result the FCThermalManagementResult: units (per role: design, n_parallel, family, geometry), mdot_coolant, mdot_ram_air, capture_area, pump_power, pump_mass, hex_mass, ram_drag, ram_drag_raw, heat_balance, candidates (design-point selection table)
    aircraft.weight.tms_result.checks tms.CheckAtPoint of the chosen hardware at every profile point and every integration point of the mission (see Checking the hardware); tms_result.violations() lists the broken limits
    aircraft.mission.thermal_profile the heat-load points the TMS is sized and rated on
    aircraft.mission.thermal_history the same quantities at every accepted integration point of the mission
    aircraft.fuelcell.P_aux_tms pump power carried by the fuel cell [W]
    aircraft.aerodynamics.extra_Cd ram-air drag increment on the polar

    Sizing at a single point¶

    The subsystem can also be used on its own, e.g. for trade studies at fixed conditions:

    tms = aircraft.tms                  # after configure(...) with tms_class='II'
point = {'name': 'climb', 'Q_fc': 4.0e6, 'altitude': 2000.0, 'V_inf': 100.0, 'mdot_h2': 0.05}
res = tms.SizeAtPoint(point)        # FCThermalManagementResult
print(res.total_mass, res.pump_power, res.ram_drag)
q_rejected, q_required = tms.RateAtPoint(res, {**point, 'altitude': 6000.0, 'V_inf': 130.0})

    examples/27_hydrogen_tms_class_ii.py compares Class I and Class II on the same aircraft, prints the design-point selection, plots the rating over the mission and shows the effect of the intake ram recovery.

    2. Heat load¶

    The fuel-cell heat is the stack heat of the Kulikovsky cell model (see Hydrogen). Mission records it along the mission in mission.thermal_profile, a list of points {name, Q_fc, altitude, V_inf (TAS), mdot_h2}:

    • the take-off/OEI point, where the fuel cell delivers the worst-case off-mission power at the take-off constraint conditions (not flown by the profile);
    • the peak of each flown segment, named Mission/<stage>, Diversion/<stage>, ....

    mission.TO_FC_Thermal_Pwr and mission.Max_FC_Thermal_Pwr are the take-off and in-flight peaks.

    3. Class I¶

    \[ W_{\text{TMS}} = \frac{\max(Q_{\text{mission peak}},\; Q_{\text{take-off}})}{\text{HEX specific power}}, \]

    with the specific power EnergyConfig.hex_specific_power_h2 (default 5000 W/kg). The take-off point is included because the fuel cell is sized there and rejects the most heat there.

    4. Class II: the FC_SPEC architecture¶

    At a design point with stack heat \(Q_{fc}\):

    1. Reactant heat. Hydrogen and cathode air enter the stack below \(T_{op}\) and absorb \(q_{\text{react}} = \dot m_{H_2}\,[h(T_{op}) - h(T_{op}-\Delta T_{H_2})] + \dot m_{\text{air}}\,c_p\,(T_{op} - T_{\text{comp}})\). The cathode air flow follows from the hydrogen flow and the air stoichiometry, and \(T_{\text{comp}}\) is the compressor delivery temperature. The coolant rejects \(Q_{\text{ext}} = Q_{fc} - q_{\text{react}}\).
    2. Coolant loop. The coolant leaves the stack at \(T_{op}\) and returns \(\Delta T_{\text{loop}}\) colder, so \(\dot m_c = Q_{\text{ext}}/(c_p\,\Delta T_{\text{loop}})\).
    3. HEX 1 (coolant → H2). The fixed duty warms the hydrogen from saturated liquid at the supply pressure to \(T_{op} - \Delta T_{H_2}\): \(q_1 = \dot m_{H_2}\,[h(T_{op}-\Delta T_{H_2}) - h_{\text{sat,liq}}]\). Enthalpies are for para-hydrogen from CoolProp, so the latent heat is included. In the ε-NTU method the hydrogen carries an effective \(c_p = \Delta h / \Delta T\).
    4. HEX 2 (coolant → ram air). This exchanger rejects the remainder \(q_2 = Q_{\text{ext}} - q_1\). The ram air enters at the free-stream total temperature and its temperature rise is \(\min(\Delta T_{\text{air}},\; \varepsilon_{\max}\,(T_{c,in} - T_{0}))\), which fixes the ram-air mass flow.
    5. Pump. \(P_{\text{pump}} = \dot V_c\,\Delta p_{\text{loop}} / (\eta_{\text{pump}}\,\eta_{\text{motor}})\); the mass is \(P/\text{sp}_{\text{pump}} + P/\text{sp}_{\text{motor}}\). The stack channels and the piping take stack_pressure_drop of the loop pressure rise; the two exchanger cores share the rest.
    6. Ram-air drag. This uses a Meredith-type momentum balance of the duct (Systems/Thermal/ram_air.py). The free stream is decelerated in the intake, which recovers a fraction \(\eta_{\text{ram}}\) of the dynamic pressure, \(p_{0,1} = p_\infty + \eta_{\text{ram}}(p_{0,\infty} - p_\infty)\). The air is heated at the core velocity (Rayleigh flow), loses the core pressure drop, and is expanded back to ambient pressure: \(D = \dot m\,(V_\infty - V_{\text{exit}})\). Negative values (net thrust) are reported in ram_drag_raw and clipped to zero in ram_drag.

    Exchanger sizing (Systems/Thermal/hex.py)¶

    For each role, three families are sized and the lightest one that respects the per-unit caps is kept:

    Family Liquid side Gas side Arrangement
    Plain tube bank (staggered, square pitch) Gnielinski / laminar Nu = 3.66 Zukauskas, with row correction; Jakob pressure drop (as given in Holman) cross-flow, gas mixed
    Annular-finned tube bank (Al fins, steel tubes) Gnielinski / laminar Nu = 3.66 Briggs & Young; Schmidt fin efficiency cross-flow, gas mixed
    Plate-fin (Al, offset-strip fins) plain channel, Gnielinski / laminar Nu = 7.54 Manglik & Bergles j and f counterflow
    • ε-NTU. The relations are the closed forms of Incropera's Table 11.3. For cross-flow with one stream mixed, the relation depends on whether the mixed (gas) stream has the smaller or the larger heat-capacity rate.
    • Layout. The design velocities (1.75 m/s for liquids, 15 m/s for gases) fix the flow areas: tube circuits, the gas face area, and the number of plate-fin layers. The required area then fixes the bank depth or the core length. The fin pitch is set from the fin diameter, so adjacent fins never overlap, and the fin densities stay within the tested range of Briggs & Young.
    • Self-consistency. U is recomputed from the real velocities of the built layout, and the area is iterated until the two agree. Sizing and rating use the same layout and correlations, so rating a unit at its own design point returns its design duty.
    • Reference area. U is referred to the gas-side area; the liquid-side and wall resistances are scaled by the area ratio.
    • Parallel units. Each role uses the fewest identical units in parallel such that one unit respects max_weight_per_unit and max_volume_per_unit. If even max_hex_units units cannot, the units are allowed to exceed the caps and a warning is issued.
    • Pressure-drop limits. The air-side pressure drop is limited to the recovered dynamic pressure, \(\eta_{\text{ram}}\,q_\infty\). The H2 side is limited to 10 kPa. On the liquid side the two cores are in series and share the loop pressure rise minus the stack and piping drop, \(\Delta p_{\text{loop}} - \Delta p_{\text{stack+piping}}\): HEX 1 may use all of it, HEX 2 what HEX 1 leaves.

    The drawings below show one unit of each role of the hydrogen example (to scale; large cores are shown as a detail), generated with PhlyGreen.Systems.Thermal.plotting.draw_hex:

    from PhlyGreen.Systems.Thermal.plotting import draw_hex, plot_tms_rating
for unit in aircraft.weight.tms_result.units:
    draw_hex(unit.design, n_parallel=unit.n_parallel).savefig(f"{unit.role}.png")

    Design point¶

    The hardware must reject the heat at every mission point, and the point with the most heat is not necessarily the one that needs the heaviest exchangers. tms.SizeForMission(profile):

    1. sizes the TMS at every point of thermal_profile carrying at least half of the peak heat;
    2. rates each geometry at every point, with the hardware fixed: the coolant flow is set by the pump, the air flow by the intake capture area (\(\dot m = \rho V A_{\text{capture}}\), flap fully open), and U is recomputed at the new flows;
    3. keeps the lightest geometry whose worst shortfall is within 2 %. If none qualifies, it keeps the least-short one and issues a warning;
    4. rates the kept geometry at every accepted integration point of the mission (mission.thermal_history). A point inside a segment can carry less heat than the segment peak and still need more cooling, for example at a lower speed. If the hardware falls short there by more than 2 %, the worst such point joins the candidates and the rated points, and steps 1-3 are repeated (at most three times). When the hardware is not short anywhere along the mission, this step changes nothing.

    In the hydrogen example only the first climb segment gives hardware that rejects the heat everywhere. Lighter geometries sized at higher, faster points fall short at the slow early climb; heavier ones (take-off, Climb2) are not needed.

    Checking the hardware along the mission¶

    tms.CheckAtPoint(result, point) gives the operating state of the fixed hardware at one point. The coolant flow is the pump flow. When the open intake would reject more heat than required, the flap throttles the air flow to the one that rejects the required heat. The check reports:

    • the heat rejected against the heat to reject;
    • the coolant-side pressure drop of the two cores against the loop budget \(\Delta p_{\text{loop}} - \Delta p_{\text{stack+piping}}\);
    • the air-side pressure drop of the ram-air core against the recovered dynamic pressure;
    • thermal choking of the duct;
    • the duct drag of the momentum balance, and the drag the aircraft charges at that point through the constant ΔCd;
    • the lowest coolant-side wall temperature of the cores (the coolant leaving a core against the cold stream entering it) against the coolant freezing point. The freezing point is CoolProp's T_freeze of the coolant (258.6 K for INCOMP::MEG-30%). Fluids without CoolProp freezing data are not checked.

    The outputs are sorted by what the model can resolve:

    Output Treated as Reason
    heat (shortfall above 2 %) violation the hardware is sized against it with the same correlations
    core coolant pressure drop vs loop budget violation same: a sizing constraint of the cores
    ram-air core pressure drop vs recovered dynamic pressure violation same
    thermal choking of the duct violation same
    coolant-side wall below the freezing point risk the wall estimate uses core-average film resistances and single-stream hydrogen properties at the cold end; it flags the risk but cannot decide a margin of a few kelvin
    duct drag, point by point not checked the aircraft carries the duct drag as a constant ΔCd. It is compared over the mission as a drag work (ram_drag_work vs ram_drag_work_accounted). On the reference design the local excess at the start of climb is 71 N, 0.6 % of the aircraft drag, below the accuracy of the drag polar

    At the converged design, tms.CheckMission runs this check at every profile point and every integration point, stores it in tms_result.checks and integrates the two drag works. tms_result.violations() and tms_result.risks() list one message per broken limit or flagged risk; each one is issued as a RuntimeWarning, and tms.report() prints them with the drag-work line. fly_off_design in examples/30_off_design_mission.py runs the same check on the flown off-design mission: broken limits are violations, risks go to risks.

    Coupling to the aircraft¶

    Weight._fuel_cell_and_tms_closure is shared by Weight.Hydrogen and Weight.FuelCellBattery, so a battery-free fuel-cell + battery design is identical to the hydrogen one. Each weight iteration repeats the following until the fuel-cell power, the pump power and the drag increment all converge:

    1. fly the mission;
    2. size the TMS from the thermal profile;
    3. set the pump power as fuelcell.P_aux_tms, a constant electrical load. It is added to the electrical demand at every operating point and to the stack rating, so its hydrogen is flown and the stack is sized for it;
    4. set the ram drag as aerodynamics.extra_Cd, \(D / (q_{\text{design}}\,S)\) with \(S = W_{TO}\,g/(W/S)\). A constant ΔCd scales the duct drag with the dynamic pressure at every flight condition;
    5. resize the fuel cell.

    The constraint diagram is computed before the weight loop, so the duct drag does not move the design point (W/S, P/W). It does act on the mission, the take-off and the OEI powers.

    Left: the TMS mass grows smoothly with the heat load (the per-unit caps change the number of units, not the trend). Right: the net duct drag at the design point against the intake ram recovery. With an ideal intake the heated air leaves faster than it entered (Meredith effect) and the duct gives a small thrust; a few percent of lost dynamic pressure turns it into drag.

    5. Inputs (TMSConfig / TMSInput)¶

    Field Legacy key Default Meaning
    tms_class TMS Class 'I' 'I' or 'II'
    coolant TMS Coolant INCOMP::MEG-30% CoolProp coolant name
    coolant_delta_t TMS Coolant DeltaT 10 K coolant temperature drop over the loop
    max_volume_per_unit TMS Max Volume Per Unit 0.05 m³ per-unit volume cap
    max_weight_per_unit TMS Max Weight Per Unit 50 kg per-unit mass cap
    max_hex_units TMS Max HEX Units 100 max parallel units per role
    ram_air_delta_t TMS Ram Air DeltaT 50 K design ram-air temperature rise
    ram_air_max_effectiveness TMS Ram Air Max Effectiveness 0.8 cap on that rise, as a fraction of the inlet ΔT
    h2_approach_delta_t TMS H2 Approach DeltaT 30 K H2 leaves HEX 1 at \(T_{op}\) minus this
    h2_supply_pressure TMS H2 Supply Pressure 1.6 bar LH2 enters HEX 1 saturated at this pressure
    loop_pressure_drop TMS Loop Pressure Drop 2 bar pump pressure rise (stack, piping, cores)
    stack_pressure_drop TMS Stack And Piping Pressure Drop 0 bar part of the loop rise taken by the stack channels and the piping; the cores share the rest (must be below loop_pressure_drop)
    pump_efficiency TMS Pump Efficiency 0.75
    pump_motor_efficiency TMS Pump Motor Efficiency 0.95
    pump_specific_power TMS Pump Specific Power 800 W/kg
    pump_motor_specific_power TMS Pump Motor Specific Power 5000 W/kg
    ram_recovery TMS Ram Recovery 1.0 fraction of the dynamic pressure the intake recovers
    takeoff_coolant_overtemperature TMS Takeoff Coolant Overtemperature 0 K coolant/stack allowed above \(T_{op}\) at the take-off point only

    None of the defaults is calibrated against flight hardware. There is no sourced value for the stack and piping pressure drop; its default of 0 gives the whole loop rise to the cores.

    6. Limitations¶

    • Core-only mass. Headers, manifolds, ducts, piping, the coolant inventory and the mounting structure are not included.
    • No fouling resistance. The finned-bank air-side friction factor (\(f = 1.5\,Re^{-0.2}\)) is an approximation without a literature source.
    • Drag is a lower bound. With the default ideal intake (ram_recovery = 1), the Meredith heating can recover more momentum than the core loses, and the clipped drag is zero. There is no spillage or cowl drag, so use ram_recovery < 1 for a realistic duct. The aircraft carries the duct drag as a constant ΔCd fixed at the design point. Over the mission this charges more drag work than the momentum balance, while at single points (the start of climb) the momentum balance can give slightly more.
    • No fans. Take-off and low-speed points are cooled by ram air only. When take-off is the largest heat load at the lowest speed, it sizes the exchangers. Two levers reduce that penalty:
    • letting the coolant run hotter for the short take-off phase (takeoff_coolant_overtemperature; e.g. 95 °C against 80 °C nominal);
    • covering part of the take-off power with a battery ('FuelCellBattery' with a take-off phi). When the power requirement comes from the climb rather than the take-off, the climb point sizes the TMS and neither lever has an effect.
    • Coolant freezing is a flagged risk, not a design constraint. HEX 1 has a water-glycol coolant facing hydrogen at ≈ 20 K. The check compares an estimate of the lowest coolant-side wall temperature with the freezing point and reports a risk. The estimate uses the core-average film resistances and single-stream hydrogen properties, so it does not resolve the temperature field at the cold end of the core, and the sizing does not change the geometry to avoid it. A design that flags the risk needs a finer model of HEX 1 or an architecture that keeps the coolant away from the cryogenic hydrogen (an intermediate loop or a hydrogen preheater), neither of which is modelled.
    • Physics not included. Ortho/para conversion is not modelled (pure para-hydrogen enthalpies). The stack is assumed to hold \(T_{op}\) at all loads. There are no transients.
